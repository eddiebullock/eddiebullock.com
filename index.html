PhD research

I am a PhD researcher at the University of Cambridge, funded by the Medical Research Council industrial Collaborative Award in Science and Engineering scholarship.

I work on vision-language models. I am building end-to-end models that interpret social interaction, and deploying them to run on wearable glasses at low latency (100 ms). I conduct mechanistic interpretability on how these models represent a social scene. We collaborate closely with AI researchers at Stanford and Harvard.

Publications are available on Google Scholar.

AI engineering internship at Minimal

I am working on a vision-language-action model for an autonomous delivery robot. I am building the pipelines for data collection and training, fine-tuning the model for deployment, collecting synthetic data, and developing the simulation environment.

Highlights

I led Minimal World Models v1. It compiles a real city into a metric 3D simulation, cheaply enough that the world is ready before the robot arrives. This is Minato, Tokyo.

I built the open-loop and closed-loop evaluation pipelines that score the driving model in simulation before it runs on the robot.

I built the photorealism pipeline that turns the simulator into driving video, keeping the motion and the street layout so the model can train on it.

I led the application for a UK government training grant of 20,000+ GPU hours, worth £50,000.
